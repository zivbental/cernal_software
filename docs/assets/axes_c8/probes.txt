4. KIM'S GEOMETRY AGAINST THE NAIVE ONE, PAIRED BY DESIGN
  `geom` is part of the CELL key, so a within-cell z-score can never compare the two
  geometries -- every cell holds exactly one. That is a property of the cell key and
  not of the data: one trigger pair contributes TWO cells, one per geometry, built on
  the same two windows. So the comparison is available, it just has to be made one
  level up: match each design in the naive cell to the design in the Kim cell that
  shares its whole axis tuple -- same stem, closure, upper3 and lower3 -- and compare
  the matched values. Only `geom` then differs, which is what a paired test needs.

  The pair is the unit: a trigger pair is won by the geometry with the better median
  over its matched designs, and the sign test is over pairs, never over designs.

5. HOW MUCH OF THE OUTCOME IS THE TRIGGER PAIR, AND HOW MUCH THE DESIGN?
  Every other number in this file is a within-cell z-score, which SUBTRACTS the trigger
  pair on purpose so that a design decision becomes visible. That makes those numbers
  structurally unable to answer how much the pair itself decides -- the quantity was
  removed before the comparison started. So this probe uses the RAW oriented values and
  splits their variance in two: the part that lies BETWEEN trigger pairs, and the part
  that lies WITHIN one pair, across the designs built on it.

  between-pair share near 100% means the pair decides and the levers only shuffle
  designs inside a verdict already made; near 0% means the levers decide. Both
  geometries are pooled, because geometry is a design decision and not a property of
  the pair.
