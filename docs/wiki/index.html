1 cm · tissue
iGEM 2026 · TAU-Israel · the biological compiler

Every cell speaks the same language: RNA.

CERNAL reads what a cell is saying and compiles it into a genetic circuit that answers only to that cell.

Scroll to enter the cell
01 · Zoom in

A cell is a crowd of messages.

Past the membrane there is no single thing to read — thousands of RNA messages are being written, copied and read at once.

Cell A
02 · The cytoplasm

Every colour is a different gene.

Each strand is an mRNA — a gene being read. How many copies of each the cell holds is not random.

The fingerprint

CERNAL finds the unique fingerprint of each cell.

From a transcriptomic signature, line the messages up by abundance. The pattern that appears belongs to this cell type, and no other.

Same genes, different print

A target cell and its neighbour leave different prints, side by side.

Lines that glow yellow are strong in Cell A only, and lines that glow red are strong in Cell B only. That difference is exactly what a circuit can be built to recognise.

03 · Zoom to one message

Pick the RNA that tells the cells apart.

High in the target, quiet elsewhere. Everything else fades; this one transcript is where the circuit will listen.

Find a window nobody else has

A short stretch becomes the key.

CERNAL scores every window of the transcript — is it exposed enough to bind, unique, buildable? The winner is the trigger.

04 · The metaphor

A switch is a lock. Only one key turns it.

That is the whole idea. The lock sits on a gene and keeps it shut; the key is a piece of the cell's own RNA.

The lock

A hairpin that hides the start signal.

The switch is an RNA folded back on itself. Its ribosome landing site (RBS) is buried in the loop, so the gene stays silent — even though the ribosome keeps trying.

The key

The trigger finds the toehold and unzips the stem.

The trigger from the last scene matches the short free toehold. It pairs in, base by base, and pulls the hairpin open. The lock is turned.

The answer

Ribosome in, protein out.

With the RBS exposed the ribosome lands, finds AUG and reads the gene: a reporter, or a therapeutic payload — only in the cell that carries the key.

05 · Two locks in a row

One key is a signal. Two keys are a fingerprint.

Chain two locks and the gene opens only when both triggers are present: logic, written in RNA. Try it.
