Controlled Gate Panel

Sixteen A0 two-input AND gates arranged so that each row differs from another in exactly one thing: which objective chose it, which secondary-arm geometry it uses, or which trigger pair it sits on. A bench result therefore points at one cause.

The grid

4 trigger pairs × 2 geometries × 2 objectives = 16 rows, all distinct. The trigger pair is held constant across the two geometries, which is what makes the geometry comparison mean the geometry rather than the transcript.

The two geometries

naive 18nt
161-nt switch, secondary arm k2* = 18 − len_x. The default build.
Kim 20/17/AUA
165-nt switch: Kim 2019's 20-nt arm, 17-nt invasion, AUA cap.

Holding the pair across both took the right key. On (a_start, b_start) 0 of 2,078 pairs are shared, and that was reported here as structurally impossible. It is not: b_end is identical in both and b_start differs by 1 nt only because trigger B's window is 50 nt in the naive build and 49 in Kim's. Same location, different length. On (a_start, b_end, len_x), 1,020 of 1,042 are shared.

The two objectives

f1
AND-ness in kcal/mol: open(11) − min[open(00), open(01), open(10)]. A difference of free energies, bounded, and it names which state leaks.
Barrier
the activation energy alone, over the folding saddle from findpath. An upper bound — a heuristic direct path, not the minimum-barrier one.

Two, not four, and that is measured. Under pair control A_M ratio (the A_M ratio) picks the same design as opening SEP in every cell, and combined with SEP collapses onto Barrier for a structural reason: access is a property of the trigger pair, so within a cell it is a constant offset. Keeping all four gave 4 distinct designs out of 8 rows; these two give 16 of 16. Both other values are still shown on every card so the collapse can be checked.

How to read a card

paired · shut0.5unpaired · open

One slice per nucleotide, 5′ left; colour is the probability that base is unpaired across the whole folding ensemble. Teal marks the main stem's descending arms (main_z, main_pre) -- what the energy terms constrain. The AUG bulge between them is excluded on purpose: it is already open in the OFF state by design. Purple marks x*, the lock the secondary hairpin holds. Two domain tracks sit below: the main hairpin (fixed 3′ 75 nt) and the secondary hairpin, whose arms depend on len_x and the geometry.

▲ higher is better · ▼ lower is better.

open 00/01/10 ▲
cost to force the stem open with no trigger, with B alone, with A alone. Higher means firmly shut. State 10 is the one that fails -- the weakest OFF state in 400 of 400 designs checked.
ON opening cost, state 11 ▼
A cost in kcal/mol, so lower is better — not a signal level. What forcing the main stem open costs with both triggers present. The ON state must open cheaply. Capped at 4.0, calibrated to Green's measured switches, whose 77 constructs above 20-fold ON/OFF have a median of 3.07.
opening SEP ▼
AND-ness — the floor, not a ranking. Every row satisfies SEP ≤ −2.0. It is a ratio in log units, so it says nothing about ON strength on its own: over the 2,559 feasible designs it correlates with open 11 at only ρ = −0.136, which is why the ON cap exists as a separate filter.
A_M ratio ▲
A_M ratio. Unbounded: a near-zero denominator inflates it. Still the strongest single term against Green.
Barrier ▼
activation energy, kcal/mol. An upper bound, from a heuristic direct path.
combined ▲
percentile aggregate of the A_M ratio and the Barrier, weighted 0.60 / 0.40 — percentiles rather than a sum, because sd(access) 7.0 and sd(Barrier) 4.4 against sd(SEP) 2.9 mean a plain sum is decided by the two large terms. This is what the panel ranks on.
combined with SEP ▲
the same aggregate with opening SEP in the A_M ratio's place. Shown but not used, so the claim that opening SEP drags the aggregate down can be checked on each card rather than taken on trust.
RBS(11) ▼
cost of opening the whole 11-nt Shine–Dalgarno at once in the ON state, kcal/mol. Its own metric, not part of A_M — A_M spans main_z + AUG + main_pre and the RBS loop is a different domain. Deliberately the joint form and not a per-base mean: the ribosome needs the whole site open together. Only state 11 is asked, because in the other three the RBS sits open in its loop by design. Measured 0.42 to 4.73 across the nine, so it discriminates.
A_M ratio ▲
A_M(11) / max(A_M OFF), where A_M is the mean per-base unpaired probability over main_z + AUG + main_pre (18 nt, identical in both architectures). A mean and not a joint probability because over 18 nt the joint is ~1e-22 for any arm.
x*, four terms ▲
the lock's job changes per state, so these are four different quantities: x* paired with sw_x in 00 and 10 (the lock holds), x* unpaired in 01 (B freed it), x* paired with trigger A in 11 (A took the freed site). All four read higher-is-right.
x* re-pairs with, 01
where x* goes once B has freed it. B releases the secondary stem completely — the x*-to-sw_x term is 0.000 — so the weak 01 figure is not a failure to open. x* is freed and immediately re-paired, mostly with the linker (up to 0.66) or sec_z (up to 0.43), which is why it is not available to A.
A engages the main stem ▲
share of trigger A's contacts landing in the stem's ascending arm (main_pre_star + bulge_star + k1_star), with and without B. Read this instead of the cooperativity.
ΔG bind ▼
duplex energy per trigger, and for A given B. Their difference is not a cooperativity: A binds the secondary region without B and the main stem with it, so the contact overlap is 0.00 and the two terms price different events.

What this panel already shows

The geometry verdict depends on which objective you ask — and that is the finding. With the trigger pair held fixed across 22 pairs that carry gating designs in both geometries:

judged by naive 18nt winsKim 20/17/AUA wins
opening SEP · AND-ness175 gap (Kim − naive) mean +2.21, sd 2.80, median +3.01, range −2.94 to +6.71
combined · what the panel ranks on1111 a dead tie

An earlier version of this page reported "the naive 18-nt arm beats Kim in all four pairs" as the panel's headline result. On 22 pairs instead of 4 it is 17–5 rather than 4–0 — but more importantly it is an opening SEP result, and the SEP is the objective that scores −0.107 against Green's measured switches. Judged by combined the two geometries are indistinguishable. Both are in the panel because a claim that changes sign with the judge is a claim the bench has to settle.

Caveats that matter. The sweep is still running: 35,331 of 336,320 designs scored, 2,559 feasible, 1,026 gating — so cells are small and every rate here should be read against its count. The 165nt = Kim label is an inference from switch length, since the folded shards do not record which flag produced them. Off-target is not joined to the scorer — of 399 flagged trigger pairs, 23 fire the gate alone and 113 block the real triggers, so it belongs as a hard filter before ordering. And the mCherry codon-shuffled reporter variants are not yet built into the sequences to order.

What would be ordered

Every sequence on this page with its coordinates in the original mCherry transcript. Differences from the original are marked, so a recoded window can be checked base by base against the sequence it replaces.

The ordering sheet. This writes the rows currently shown into the layout of AND set 1.xlsx: the switch and both triggers split into their domains, the four full mCherry transcripts with every changed stretch marked inside the sequence, and a block of sanity checks. Every check is a formula — it recomputes from the cells in the file, so a wrong cell fails in your own Excel rather than being blessed by whatever wrote it. The reverse-complement checks need Excel 2021 or 365.

It works in both places and takes a different route in each. Opened as a local file at report/panel.html it downloads straight away; in the published report a page cannot start a download by itself, so the workbook goes through the viewer's own save prompt and you confirm it there.
